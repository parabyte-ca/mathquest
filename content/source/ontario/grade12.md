# Grade 12: Six courses (2007 curriculum, still in force)

The 2007 Grades 11-12 document remains in effect as of fall 2026. No revised Grade 12 math curriculum has been announced. Assessment rules from 2026-27: written final exam 25% and attendance/participation 10% for Grades 11-12 (workplace-preparation courses are exempt from the exam requirement).

## Prerequisites and pathways

| Course | Code | Prerequisite |
|---|---|---|
| Advanced Functions, University | **MHF4U** | MCR3U, or MCT4C |
| Calculus and Vectors, University | **MCV4U** | MHF4U, taken before or at the same time |
| Mathematics of Data Management, University | **MDM4U** | MCR3U or MCF3M |
| Mathematics for College Technology, College | **MCT4C** | MCF3M or MCR3U |
| Foundations for College Mathematics, College | **MAP4C** | MBF3C (designed for it; other pathways possible) |
| Mathematics for Work and Everyday Life, Workplace | **MEL4E** | MEL3E |

Routing notes for the app:
- MHF4U is the gateway to MCV4U; offer them in that order, or in parallel only after MHF4U strand D (rates of change) is mastered.
- MDM4U is less algebraic; it suits students coming from either MCR3U or MCF3M.
- MCT4C opens a second route into MHF4U for college-stream students.

## MHF4U: Advanced Functions

**A. Exponential and Logarithmic Functions**
- A1: relationship between exponential and logarithmic expressions; evaluate logarithms; laws of logarithms to simplify numeric expressions.
- A2: key features of graphs of logarithmic functions; connect numeric, graphical and algebraic forms; solve related problems graphically; transformations of y = log x.
- A3: solve exponential and simple logarithmic equations in one variable algebraically, including real-world problems (pH, decibels, Richter scale, growth and decay).

**B. Trigonometric Functions**
- B1: meaning and application of radian measure; convert degrees and radians; exact values for special angles in radians.
- B2: connect trig ratios to graphs and equations of sin, cos, tan and the reciprocal functions csc, sec, cot; sinusoidal models y = a sin(k(x − d)) + c in radians.
- B3: solve trigonometric equations (linear and quadratic in a trig function, on a given interval); prove identities, including compound-angle and double-angle formulas.

**C. Polynomial and Rational Functions**
- C1: key features of polynomial functions (degree, leading coefficient, end behaviour, zeros and their order, symmetry: even/odd); power functions; transformations; connect numeric, graphical and algebraic forms.
- C2: key features of rational functions (vertical and horizontal asymptotes, holes, domain, range, intervals of increase/decrease); reciprocals of linear and quadratic functions; ratio of two linear functions.
- C3: solve polynomial and simple rational equations graphically and algebraically; factor theorem, remainder theorem, long and synthetic division; families of polynomials.
- C4: solve polynomial and simple rational inequalities (factor tables, number lines, interval notation).

**D. Characteristics of Functions**
- D1: average rate of change over an interval and instantaneous rate of change at a point, numerically and graphically (secant to tangent).
- D2: combine functions by addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and composition; properties of the results; f(g(x)) and inverse checks.
- D3: compare characteristics of function families; model and reason with functions, including problems not solvable by standard algebra (use graphing technology).

## MCV4U: Calculus and Vectors

**A. Rate of Change**
- A1: average vs. instantaneous rate of change; slopes of secants and tangents; the concept of the limit; first principles.
- A2: graph derivatives of polynomial, sinusoidal and exponential functions; connect a function and its derivative numerically, graphically and algebraically; d/dx sin x = cos x, d/dx eˣ = eˣ.
- A3: verify and apply derivative rules (power, constant multiple, sum/difference, product, quotient, chain) to polynomial, sinusoidal, exponential, rational and radical functions and simple combinations.

**B. Derivatives and Their Applications**
- B1: connect key features of a function to its first and second derivatives (increasing/decreasing, local max/min, concavity, points of inflection); curve sketching.
- B2: solve problems, including optimization and rate-of-change problems from real-world contexts, including building the model.

**C. Geometry and Algebra of Vectors**
- C1: vectors in 2-space and 3-space, geometric and algebraic (Cartesian) forms; magnitude and direction.
- C2: operations: addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication, dot product, cross product; properties; applications (force, velocity, work, torque, projections).
- C3: geometric meaning of a single linear equation and of systems in 2-space and 3-space; configurations of lines and planes.
- C4: lines and planes as scalar, vector and parametric equations; distances and intersections (line-line, line-plane, plane-plane, three planes).

## MDM4U: Mathematics of Data Management

**A. Counting and Probability**
- A1: probability of an event or combination of events for discrete sample spaces; complement, mutually exclusive, independent and dependent events; conditional probability; Venn and tree diagrams.
- A2: permutations and combinations (n!, nPr, nCr), including identical items; Pascal's triangle; apply to probability.

**B. Probability Distributions**
- B1: discrete probability distributions; expected value; uniform, binomial and hypergeometric distributions, numerically, graphically and algebraically.
- B2: continuous probability distributions; connect to discrete; standard deviation; normal distribution features; z-scores; normal approximation to the binomial.

**C. Organization of Data for Analysis**
- C1: role of data in statistical studies; characteristics of well-designed studies, including randomization and sources of bias.
- C2: characteristics of a good sample; sampling techniques (simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster, multi-stage); primary data collection; collect and organize data.

**D. Statistical Analysis**
- D1: one-variable data: measures of central tendency and spread (range, quartiles, IQR, variance, standard deviation), percentiles, histograms, box plots, outliers.
- D2: two-variable data: scatter plots, correlation coefficient r, line of best fit by regression, residuals, causation vs. correlation, hidden and lurking variables.
- D3: data management in the media, advertising and occupations; evaluate statistical claims; margin of error and confidence statements.

**E. Culminating Data Management Investigation**
- E1: design and carry out an investigation integrating the course.
- E2: communicate findings and critique others' investigations.
- App note: support this as a guided multi-step project, not as scored items.

## MCT4C: Mathematics for College Technology

**A. Exponential Functions**
- A1: solve exponential equations graphically, including real-world problems.
- A2: solve exponential equations algebraically using common bases and logarithms.

**B. Polynomial Functions**
- B1: recognize and evaluate polynomial functions; key features of graphs; solve problems with graphs.
- B2: connect numeric, graphical and algebraic representations.
- B3: solve polynomial equations by factoring; connect functions and formulas; rearrange formulas used in technology fields.

**C. Trigonometric Functions**
- C1: trig ratios for angles less than 360°; primary trig ratios, sine law, cosine law.
- C2: connect numeric, graphical and algebraic representations of sinusoidal functions.
- C3: sinusoidal models of periodic phenomena, including real-world problems.

**D. Applications of Geometry**
- D1: represent, add and subtract vectors; vector models (forces, navigation).
- D2: problems with 2-D shapes and 3-D figures from real-world applications.
- D3: circle properties (chords, tangents, arcs, sectors, inscribed angles) and related problems.

## MAP4C: Foundations for College Mathematics

**A. Mathematical Models**
- A1: powers with rational exponents; simplify expressions with exponents; solve exponential equations graphically and with common bases.
- A2: describe trends from graphs; compare graphs using initial conditions and rates of change; model relationships graphically and algebraically.
- A3: connect formulas with linear, quadratic and exponential relations; solve with formulas; modelling in occupations.

**B. Personal Finance**
- B1: annuities, including mortgages; solve with technology (TVM solver, amortization tables).
- B2: owning vs. renting accommodation; associated costs.
- B3: design, justify and adjust budgets for individuals and families from case studies.

**C. Geometry and Trigonometry**
- C1: measurement and geometry problems from real-world applications.
- C2: optimal dimensions of 2-D shapes and 3-D figures (maximum area or volume, minimum perimeter or surface area).
- C3: primary trig ratios of acute and obtuse angles, sine law, cosine law; trigonometry in occupations.

**D. Data Management**
- D1: collect, analyse and summarize two-variable data; interpret and draw conclusions.
- D2: data management in the media, advertising and occupations.

## MEL4E: Mathematics for Work and Everyday Life

All strands rely on proportional reasoning.

**A. Reasoning with Data**
- A1: collect, organize, represent and make inferences from data; related applications.
- A2: determine and represent probability; interpret its applications (games, weather, insurance).

**B. Personal Finance**
- B1: owning vs. renting accommodation and associated costs.
- B2: interpret, design and adjust household budgets from case studies.
- B3: the process of filing a personal income tax return; applications of personal finance math.

**C. Applications of Measurement**
- C1: estimate and measure in metric and imperial; convert within and between systems.
- C2: measurement and design problems; scale drawings and scale models; budget a household improvement.
- C3: proportional relationships; consequences of proportional-reasoning errors; work and everyday problems.
