# Grade 11 — Four pathways (2007 curriculum)

Prerequisites: MCR3U requires MPM2D. MCF3M accepts MPM2D or MFM2P. MBF3C requires MFM2P. MEL3E requires Grade 9 math or a Grade 10 LDCC math course.

Destinations: MCR3U → MHF4U → MCV4U (also opens MDM4U). MCF3M → MCT4C, MAP4C or MDM4U. MBF3C → MAP4C. MEL3E → MEL4E.

## MCR3U — Functions (University)

**A. Characteristics of Functions**
- Function vs. relation (vertical-line test); function notation; domain and range for f(x) = x, x², √x, 1/x.
- Inverses: reflection in y = x; inverse domain and range; algebraic inverse.
- Transformations y = af(k(x − d)) + c.
- Number of zeros using the discriminant; max/min by completing the square; linear-quadratic intersections.
- Simplify polynomial, radical (√ab = √a√b) and rational expressions, stating restrictions; test equivalence.

**B. Exponential Functions**
- Rational exponents; simplify expressions with integer and rational exponents.
- Properties: asymptote, domain and range.
- Distinguish from linear and quadratic using constant ratios and finite differences.
- Transformations; change of base.
- Growth and decay models.

**C. Discrete Functions**
- Recursive sequences; Fibonacci; Pascal's triangle and binomial expansion.
- Arithmetic and geometric sequences and series: tₙ = a + (n − 1)d and tₙ = ar^(n−1), and their sums.
- Simple interest ↔ linear; compound interest ↔ exponential; A = P(1 + i)ⁿ; solving for n with a TVM solver.
- Ordinary simple annuities: future value, present value, payment.

**D. Trigonometric Functions**
- Exact values at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°; angles up to 360°.
- Reciprocal ratios sec, csc, cot.
- Prove identities using sin²x + cos²x = 1 and tan x = sin x / cos x.
- Sine and cosine law, including the ambiguous case and 3-D problems.
- Sinusoidal functions y = a sin(k(x − d)) + c: amplitude, period, phase shift; Ferris wheel and tide models.

## MCF3M — Functions and Applications (University/College)

**A. Quadratic Functions**
- Factor, including a ≠ 1, differences of squares and perfect squares.
- Quadratic formula (following the development, not reproducing it); discriminant and number of roots.
- Function notation, domain and range.
- a, h, k transformations; convert vertex ↔ standard form; complete the square with simple rational b/a; factored form.
- Curve of best fit.

**B. Exponential Functions**
- Rational exponents numerically only; exponent rules on numeric expressions; exponential graph properties.
- Simple vs. compound interest; A = P(1 + i)ⁿ; TVM solver for i and n; annuities.

**C. Trigonometric Functions**
- Right-triangle and two-right-triangle problems; sine and cosine law in acute triangles only.
- The sine function only, with a sin x, sin x + c and sin(x − d) transformations.

**Compared with MCR3U, MCF3M omits:** inverses, radicals, rational expressions, sequences and series, identities, the ambiguous case, cosine function transformations, and the k parameter.

## MBF3C — Foundations for College Mathematics (College)

**A. Mathematical Models**
- Quadratic tables and graphs; a, h, k in y = a(x − h)² + k; expand; factor ax² + bx + c only when a = 1 or a is a common factor; solve by factoring or graphing.
- Zero and negative exponents; numeric exponent rules; graph y = 2^x; exponential applications such as a ball's bounce height h = 2(0.6)ⁿ.

**B. Personal Finance**
- B1: simple vs. compound interest; A = P(1 + i)ⁿ; I = A − P; TVM solver.
- B2: compare financial-institution services; the cost of buying on credit.
- B3: costs of owning and operating a vehicle.

**C. Geometry and Trigonometry**
- C1: represent 2-D shapes and 3-D figures from real-world applications; solve design problems.
- C2: sine and cosine law in acute triangles.

**D. Data Management**
- D1: one-variable data: collect, organize, analyse, evaluate.
- D2: probability and its applications.

## MEL3E — Mathematics for Work and Everyday Life (Workplace)

All three strands rely on proportional reasoning. (Detail beyond the strand headers and the items below leans on secondary sources — verify against dcp.edu.gov.on.ca before quoting.)

**A. Earning and Purchasing**
- A1: remuneration types (salary, commission, piecework, benefits, vacation pay).
- A2: payroll deductions; gross vs. net pay; purchasing power.
- A3: discounts, sale prices, after-tax costs, making change, unit price / best buy.

**B. Saving, Investing, and Borrowing**
- Savings options; simple interest; compound interest by repeated simple-interest calculation for no more than 6 periods.
- Credit and debit cards; loans; credit ratings.

**C. Transportation and Travel**
- Vehicle fixed and variable costs; buying vs. leasing; insurance.
- Map scales; trip costs; comparing travel modes.
